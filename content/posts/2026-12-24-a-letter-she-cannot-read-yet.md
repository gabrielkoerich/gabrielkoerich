In 2024 I wrote one line in my journal: "Descobri que vou ser pai!" I found out I was going to be a father. A few weeks later we learned it was a girl, and I wrote: "A Laura ta vindo e cada dia eu amo mais ela." Laura is coming, and every day I love her more.

That October I wrote something I still think about: "o mais importante da vida são seus princípios. é isso que eu tenho que ensinar pra minha filha." The most important thing in life is your principles, and that is what I have to teach my daughter.

It was easy to write. It is harder to know where to start.

## The letter is not mine

I keep a letter in my notes under the title "Um presente pra minha filha", a gift for my daughter. I found it in Portuguese on [Mises Brasil](https://mises.org.br/article/863/um-presente-de-natal-definitivo-para-a-minha-filha) and saved it as the first principle I want her to have. Only while writing this post did I learn that it is a translation. [Harry Browne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Browne) wrote it in English, as a Christmas present for his own daughter in 1966, when she was nine.

Browne starts with the problem every parent has in December. He could give her books, games or clothes, and she would like them for a few months. He wanted to give her something that would last, something that would make her remember him every Christmas. So he gave her one idea, which took him years to understand:

> No one owes you anything.

The rest of the letter unpacks it, and it is worth reading in full at the link above. In short:

- Nobody is living for you. Each person lives for themselves, and their happiness is theirs.
- Nobody has to love you. If someone does, it is because something in you makes them happy. Find out what it is and grow it.
- Friends are with you because they feel good with you, not because they owe it. Some people will even be cruel, and once you stop expecting kindness as a right, you learn to stay away from them.
- You owe yourself one thing: to be the best person you can be. Never demand anything from anyone. Earn it.

Then he tells her his own story. For years he believed there were things he had a right to just for being born, and he wore himself out trying to collect them. The day he understood that the world owed him nothing, a weight came off, and every relationship he had got better. He ends by asking her to reread the letter every Christmas, because its meaning gets clearer each year.

## Why it stayed with me

The letter cuts in a direction he does not spell out. If no one owes my daughter anything, then she does not owe me anything either. Not her love, not her time, not her attention when she is older. Whatever she gives me, I will have to earn, the same way he tells her to earn everything else.

That part is uncomfortable, because I know how I did on it. At the end of 2025 I wrote: "Esse foi o ano mais difícil da minha vida. A Laura foi a melhor coisa que me aconteceu, mas eu não consegui dar a atenção real que eu queria a ela." It was the hardest year of my life. Laura was the best thing that happened to me, and I could not give her the real attention I wanted to.

Under that entry I wrote five lines, a plan more than a resolution:

> Aceitar. Pedir desculpa. Me perdoar. Aprender. Não parar.

Accept. Apologize. Forgive myself. Learn. Do not stop.

My plan for 2026 started with the same thing in English: take care of my family, spend more time with my family. A few lines down: "I can't die. Everybody depends on me."

## The gift

She is too young to read any of this. This year she carried the rings at her godparents' wedding.

When she can read, I want to give her two things at Christmas. Browne's letter, with his name on it, because it is his and it says the idea better than I do. And a short one from me, saying that I believe it, that it applies to me too, and that I am still trying to earn my place in her life every year.

Until then, this post is where I keep both.

Merry Christmas, Laura.
